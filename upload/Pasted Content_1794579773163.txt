MASTER DEVELOPMENT PROMPT
Physiotherapy Clinic Management System: GMRS
1. Project Overview
Build a modern, professional, full-stack Physiotherapy Clinic Management System designed to manage the daily operations and clinical workflows of a physiotherapy clinic.
The application must manage the complete patient journey, starting from patient registration and appointment booking through clinical assessment, diagnosis, treatment planning, therapy sessions, progress reviews, billing, and eventual discharge.
The system must also support staff management, clinic administration, insurance configuration, packages, reporting and operational dashboards.
This is a real clinic management application, not a static UI prototype. All screens, forms, navigation, business rules, database relationships and backend operations must be implemented and connected.
The application should be designed with future multi-clinic support in mind, even if the initial deployment serves one clinic.
Primary objectives
* Centralize patient records.
* Manage appointments and therapist availability.
* Maintain structured clinical documentation.
* Track patient treatment journeys.
* Manage therapy sessions and rehabilitation progress.
* Support PAYG, package and private insurance billing.
* Track clinic revenue and outstanding balances.
* Monitor therapist performance and workload.
* Provide management with operational and clinical reports.
* Maintain secure, auditable patient and financial records.

Architecture principles
* Keep the frontend and backend as separate applications.
* All business rules must be implemented and enforced in NestJS.
* The frontend must communicate with the backend through documented REST APIs.
* Do not connect the frontend directly to PostgreSQL.
* Use Prisma for database access.
* Use database transactions for operations involving multiple related records.
* Use reusable frontend components.
* Organize both applications by feature/module.
* Keep business logic separate from controllers and UI components.
* Use environment variables for application configuration.
* Implement centralized error handling and consistent API responses.
* Document APIs using Swagger.
Do not make a final decision about file storage providers or deployment infrastructure yet. Keep those areas abstracted so they can be configured later.

12. Therapy Session Management
Therapy sessions represent the actual treatment delivered to the patient.
They are separate from appointments.
One appointment can generate one therapy session for the initial implementation.
Session creation
Therapy sessions should normally be created from attended appointments.
Automatically populate:
* Patient.
* Treatment episode.
* Appointment.
* Assigned therapist.
* Session date.
Session documentation
Capture:
* Session date.
* Pain level.
* Blood pressure.
* Other relevant vital signs.
* Interventions delivered.
* Treatment duration.
* Patient response.
* Clinical observations.
* Additional notes.
* Therapist signature.
The therapist should document what actually happened, not simply copy the planned treatment interventions.
Session statuses
* Planned.
* In Progress.
* Completed.
* Cancelled.
* No-show.
Allow the therapist to save an incomplete session as a draft or in-progress record.
Only completed therapy sessions should count towards progress review thresholds and therapist completed-session statistics.
Important distinction
An appointment may be completed without a therapy session being completed.
Examples:
* Patient attended and received treatment: completed appointment and completed therapy session.
* Patient did not attend: appointment marked no-show, no completed therapy session.
* Patient attended but treatment was postponed: attended/no treatment, no completed therapy session.
* Patient cancelled: cancelled appointment, no completed therapy session.

13. Progress Review Management
Progress reviews evaluate patient improvement and treatment effectiveness.
The default review threshold is every 15 completed therapy sessions.
A therapist can initiate an earlier review at any time.
The session count must reset for the next review cycle after a completed formal review.
Only completed therapy sessions count. Booked, cancelled, missed and incomplete sessions must not count.
Progress review sections
A. Previous clinical information
Display:
* Initial assessment.
* Baseline measurements.
* Diagnosis.
* Treatment objectives.
* Rehabilitation milestones.
* Previous treatment plan.
B. Updated clinical assessment
Capture:
* Current pain levels.
* Current ROM.
* Muscle strength.
* Functional ability.
* Vital signs where relevant.
* New clinical findings.
C. Baseline comparison
Display original measurements beside current measurements.
Examples:
* Pain: 8/10 → 4/10.
* Knee flexion: 90° → 115°.
* Muscle strength: 2/5 → 4/5.
* Walking difficulty: Severe → Mild.
D. Objective and milestone evaluation
Evaluate every objective and milestone.
Statuses:
* Achieved.
* Partially Achieved.
* Not Achieved.
* No Longer Applicable.
E. Therapist summary
Capture:
* Improvements.
* Deterioration.
* Patient response.
* Clinical observations.
* Recommendations.
F. Review decision
Allow the therapist to:
* Continue existing plan.
* Modify treatment interventions.
* Modify objectives.
* Adjust session frequency.
* Revise diagnosis where clinically justified.
* Refer for further investigation.
* Discharge the patient.
When the treatment plan changes, create a new version.

14. Discharge Management
Discharge must be therapist-led.
The system may identify patients who appear ready for discharge, but must not automatically discharge them.
Discharge reasons
* Treatment objectives achieved.
* Significant improvement.
* Patient requested to stop.
* Referral to another healthcare provider.
* Treatment no longer clinically indicated.
* Patient unable or unwilling to continue.
* Other.
A patient request to stop treatment should initiate a clinical review rather than automatically closing the episode.
Patients who stop attending must not be classified as successfully recovered.
Discharge workflow
1. Therapist initiates discharge.
2. Complete final clinical assessment.
3. Compare baseline and final measurements.
4. Evaluate objectives.
5. Evaluate rehabilitation milestones.
6. Select discharge reason.
7. Prepare discharge summary.
8. Enter home exercise recommendations.
9. Add follow-up or referral arrangements.
10. Therapist signs off.
11. Close the treatment episode.

* Recording payments.
* Processing refunds.
* Revising treatment plans.

25. Important Business Rules
The following rules are mandatory.
1. One patient can have multiple treatment episodes.
2. Each treatment episode maintains its own clinical records.
3. Appointments and therapy sessions are separate entities.
4. A completed appointment does not automatically mean a completed therapy session.
5. Only completed therapy sessions count towards progress review thresholds.
6. Cancelled appointments and no-shows must not count.
7. Attended appointments without treatment must not count.
8. Progress reviews default to every 15 completed sessions.
9. The review counter resets after each completed formal review.
10. Early progress reviews must be supported.
11. Treatment plans must have version history.
12. Historical diagnoses must be retained when diagnoses change.
13. Completed clinical records must not be silently overwritten.
14. Discharge is therapist-led.
15. Discharge closes a treatment episode, not the patient.
16. Outstanding payments must not block clinical discharge.
17. Package sessions are deducted only after qualifying completed therapy sessions.
18. Package deductions must be reversible through auditable transactions.
19. Insurance rates must be configurable by provider.
20. Historical invoices must preserve the rates originally applied.
21. Therapist remuneration rules must be configurable.
22. Reports must distinguish unique patients from total completed sessions.
23. Every protected backend operation must validate the user's permissions and clinic access.
24. Financial transactions must be protected against duplicate processing.
25. Clinical and financial history must remain auditable.

26. Clinical Workflow
Implement the following end-to-end workflow.
Patient Registration ↓ Appointment Booking ↓ Patient Attendance ↓ Initial Clinical Assessment ↓ Diagnosis ↓ Treatment Plan Creation ↓ Treatment Plan Activation ↓ Therapy Sessions ↓ Progress Review after every 15 completed sessions ↓ Clinical Decision ↓ Continue / Modify / Refer / Discharge ↓ Discharge Summary ↓ Close Treatment Episode
Support these alternative paths:
* A patient can be placed on hold.
* A therapist can initiate an early progress review.
* A diagnosis can be provisional.
* A treatment plan can be revised.
* A patient can be referred for further investigation.
* A patient can request to stop treatment.
* A patient can discontinue treatment.
* A patient can return later for a new treatment episode.
